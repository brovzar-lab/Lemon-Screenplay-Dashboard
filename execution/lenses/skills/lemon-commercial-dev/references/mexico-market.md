# Mexico Market Module: Audiences, Money, and the Commercial Score

Read this when segmenting the audience, building the profit thesis, or assigning the Commercial Score.

## Audience Segmentation

Never say "Mexican audiences" as if it were one creature with a Cinépolis card. Name the segment and its behavior:

1. **Familia de fin de semana**: the family outing block, largest theatrical segment. Buys broad comedy, animation, PG-13 horror-lite. Extremely title-and-trailer driven. Ticket multiplier: they buy 4-6 at a time.
2. **Jóvenes de plaza (15-24)**: teens and college crowds at mall multiplexes. THE horror audience. Go in groups, decide same-day, driven by TikTok clips and dares ("no vas a aguantar"). B15 rating is their sweet spot.
3. **Parejas (25-40)**: date night. Romantic comedy, comedy thriller, prestige horror. Streaming-first unless the event factor is strong.
4. **Público godín / clase media urbana**: working adults, the Nosotros los Nobles core. Class comedy and recognizable-life comedy over-index here.
5. **Provincia / interior**: often underestimated, decisive for broad comedy and folklore horror. Regional identity content over-performs; CDMX-insider satire underperforms.
6. **Streaming-first households**: will never buy the ticket but drive completion rates and platform value. Some concepts are worth more here; say so honestly.
7. **US Hispanic / diaspora**: extends theatrical and platform value for the right titles (family comedy, folklore horror). Note when a concept travels north.

For every pitch: primary segment, secondary segment, and the honest size. A concept for segment 3 only is a streaming play, not a wide theatrical bet.

## Business Models (the profit thesis must name one)

1. **Theatrical ownership**: Lemon risks capital, owns upside. Needs segments 1, 2, or 4 at scale. Comedy and horror are the only genres where Mexican films reliably win wide theatrical.
2. **Streamer commission**: platform pays cost plus fee. Low risk, capped upside, no IP retention usually. Right answer for concepts with narrow theatrical audiences but strong completion appeal.
3. **Presales / MG**: sales agent and territory presales de-risk the budget. Works for horror with export imagery.
4. **Co-production**: split cost and territory (Spain via Lemon's structures, LatAm partners). Right when the concept genuinely plays in both territories, wrong when it dilutes the Mexican specificity that made it work.
5. **Remake / format play**: the concept's highest value may be the premise itself sold outward. High-concept comedy premises especially. Flag when remake value exceeds production value.
6. **Television / series sale**: when format diagnosis says series, the buyer is a platform and the economics change entirely.
7. **Franchise exploitation**: sequels, spin-offs, universe. Claim it only when the mythology or engine is genuinely renewable.

## High Gross vs High Return (worked instinct)

- A 25M MXN horror with segment-2 heat and export presales can return 3-5x. That beats a 120M MXN "important" film that needs a record-breaking opening to break even.
- Budget follows audience, never ambition. If the realistic audience is 1.5M admissions, the budget must make money at 1.5M admissions.
- Marketing spend is part of the investment. A concept that "needs educating" costs double to open. Concepts that market themselves (title, image, premise) earn a scoring premium.

## Budget Bands (rough, MXN, for production shape)

- **Contained**: under 20M. Single-location horror, small-cast comedy. Highest ROI potential in the market.
- **Standard commercial**: 20-45M. The Mexican hit comedy zone and premium horror zone.
- **Elevated**: 45-80M. Needs stars, a presold concept, or co-production money. Justify explicitly.
- **Event**: 80M+. Almost never justified for an unproven concept. Default skepticism.

## Marketing Instruments to Evaluate at Pitch Stage

- **Title**: does the title sell alone? (Mirreyes vs Godínez did.) A weak title is fixable; note it and suggest directions (or route to titulo-comercial).
- **Poster test**: one image that communicates genre and premise.
- **Trailer**: three visible moments minimum. Comedy trailers must be funny; horror trailers need the image and the dread mechanic.
- **Social mechanic**: what gets clipped, memed, dared, quoted? Horror dares and comedy quotables are free media.
- **Star slots**: which roles are castable star vehicles that expand the audience, and does the budget band survive them?

## Commercial Score Rubric (0-100)

- Hook strength (Hit Test, one-sentence sale): 20
- Audience size and segment heat: 20
- Budget-to-upside ratio (return, not gross): 15
- Marketability (title, poster, trailer, social mechanic): 15
- Word of mouth and repeat-view potential: 10
- IP and franchise value (sequels, formats, remake rights): 10
- International travel (export, remake, diaspora): 10

Bands: 85+ exceptional, priority territory. 70-84 fundable. 55-69 rebuild territory. Below 55, the market case does not exist.

## Score Interaction Reminders

- Commercial Score high + Genre Score below 75 = Rebuild or Pass, never Advance. Marketable-sounding but unfunny/unscary is the most expensive trap in development.
- Genre Score high + Commercial Score 55-69 = usually Rebuild toward a bigger audience or Hold for packaging, the engine is worth saving.
- Both below 60 = Pass. Say why in one sentence and move on.

## Awards and Prestige (last, on purpose)

Note festival or awards potential when real (it lowers marketing cost internationally and attracts talent), but it appears at the bottom of the evaluation and never lifts a verdict. Después de Lucía going to Cannes is a real asset; expecting Cannes to sell tickets in Perisur is not a plan.
