# Horror Module: Mexican Commercial Horror

Read this before scoring any horror or hybrid-horror pitch. The job is terrifying audiences, not startling them.

## Mexican Fear Territories

Renewable sources of dread with built-in audience recognition:

- **Folklore and urban legends**: La Llorona lineage, roadside ghosts, cursed places every region already whispers about
- **Catholic imagery**: possession, desecration, faith that fails, the church that knows more than it says
- **Death rituals**: velorios, Día de Muertos inverted, what happens when the rites are done wrong
- **Witches, nahuales, brujería**: rural witchcraft with real regional texture (Catemaco, the sierra), shapeshifters, limpias gone wrong
- **Family trauma**: the inherited curse as inherited abuse, Huesera territory
- **Missing children**: Mexico's rawest real fear, handle with intent, never as decoration
- **Isolation**: the pueblo cut off, the ranch, the carretera at night, no signal
- **Abandonment and guilt**: the parent who left, the sibling nobody saved
- **Violence as ambient horror**: Tigers Are Not Afraid territory, the real monster wearing folklore's mask

## Horror Registers

Rural horror, urban horror, psychological, creature, found footage, body horror, cosmic, folk horror, religious horror, home invasion. Know which the pitch is; audience, rating, and budget follow from register.

## Canonical Comps and What They Prove

- **Kilómetro 31**: Mexican folklore plus modern production value equals domestic box office history. The bar Lemon itself set.
- **Huesera**: elevated horror with a real fear engine (motherhood as body horror) can travel internationally on a controlled budget.
- **Belzebuth**: religious horror plus border darkness, genre fans showed up.
- **Veneno para las Hadas**: children plus brujería, the classic proof that Mexican horror imagery is exportable.
- **Tigers Are Not Afraid**: real violence refracted through folklore travels globally, prestige plus genre.
- **The Orphanage**: Spanish-language horror can be a crossover event when the emotional wound and the horror are the same thing.
- **The Conjuring**: mythology with rules plus family under siege equals franchise. The commercial gold standard.
- **Hereditary / Talk to Me / Smile / The Ring**: proof that one unforgettable image or mechanic (the séance hand, the smile, the tape) does the marketing by itself.

## The Fear Engine Formula

**Engine = A specific fear the audience already carries + A monster or force that embodies exactly that fear + Rules that make the threat intelligent and escapable-in-theory + A protagonist whose wound is the fear.**

Test: state what the movie is REALLY about in one sentence under the monster. "It's about a haunting" is not an answer. "It's about a mother terrified she never wanted her child, and the thing in the walls agrees" is.

## Dread Architecture

Startles are cheap; dread is the product. An advancing pitch must imply:

1. **Anticipation over impact**: the audience knows something is coming and cannot stop it.
2. **An intelligent evil**: the threat has intent, patience, and a plan. Random hauntings score low.
3. **Uncertainty**: for a meaningful stretch, neither protagonist nor audience is sure what the rules are.
4. **Escalation with a point of no return**: a moment after which normal life is unrecoverable.
5. **The violation of a safe space**: home, church, the mother's arms. Horror that stays in the haunted location and never invades the safe one caps its dread.

## The Fear Ladder

Advancing pitches should imply climbs across this ladder, not camping on one rung:

Unease → Suspicion → Dread → Terror → Horror (the reveal that recontextualizes) → Aftermath (the image that follows the audience home)

## Proof Tests (apply all)

1. **Nightmare test**: what is the nightmare? Answerable in one sentence.
2. **Fear-beneath-the-monster test**: what real fear does the monster embody? No answer, no engine.
3. **Unforgettable image test**: name the image audiences will never forget. If the pitch does not contain or imply one, red flag.
4. **Mythology test**: do the rules make sense and hold? Can they sustain sequels without contradiction?
5. **Escalation test**: does each act imply worse, closer, more personal?
6. **Describe-one-scene test**: could a viewer describe one specific scene to a friend to convince them to go?
7. **Recommendation test**: would horror fans, the most demanding word-of-mouth engine in cinema, actually recommend it?
8. **Disturbance test**: does the concept disturb (linger for days) or merely startle (forgotten in the parking lot)?

## Common Failure Modes at Pitch Stage

- **Folklore wallpaper**: a legend name-dropped with no engine underneath. La Llorona as a costume.
- **The concept without a mythology**: scary premise, no rules, third act will collapse. Verdict language: "this horror concept has no mythology."
- **The startle reel**: a list of scare moments with no dread architecture connecting them.
- **The metaphor that ate the monster**: elevated horror so busy being about trauma it forgets to be frightening. Score genre honestly; A24 aspiration does not fill seats in Ecatepec.
- **Imported hauntings**: US suburban horror translated without Mexican fear territory. Feels dubbed.
- **The unkillable-budget creature**: a concept whose scares require VFX the budget band cannot buy. Flag under production shape.

## Rating and Audience Note

Horror is the most forgiving genre for unknown casts and the least forgiving for broken promises. A B15 rating widens the crucial teen audience; a C rating needs the extremity to be the selling point. State which the concept demands and whether that matches the profit thesis.

## Horror Score (0-100)

- Fear engine (real fear under the monster): 25
- Dread architecture and escalation: 15
- Mythology coherence and sequel-bearing rules: 15
- Unforgettable image / marketable mechanic: 15
- Mexican fear-territory specificity: 10
- Intelligent evil: 10
- Disturbance over startle (word of mouth): 10

75 is the floor for Advance. Below 60 there is no engine; Rebuild only if the fear territory or central image shows real heat, otherwise Pass.
