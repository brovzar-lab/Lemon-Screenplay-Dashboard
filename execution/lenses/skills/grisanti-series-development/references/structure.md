# Grisanti Structural Framework

Structural templates for TV series development. The **Pilot Structure** is for Episode 1. The **Episodic Structure** is the standard format for Episodes 2 and beyond.

---

## The Fundamental Chain

Regardless of format, every story follows this chain:

1. **TRIGGER INCIDENT** — Forces the character into a dilemma
2. **DILEMMA** — An impossible choice where neither option is acceptable
3. **GOAL** — The choice made in the dilemma defines the goal
4. **OBSTACLES** — Escalate until the "all is lost" moment
5. **RESOLUTION** — The central character ACTIVELY achieves the goal

Every act out reflects back to the initial goal.

---

## Pilot Structure: Teaser + 4 Acts (One-Hour Drama)

This is the current standard structure for pilot episodes.

### Teaser / Cold Open
- Set up the world
- Create empathy for the central character
- Establish the personal dilemma and/or the Series Trigger
- Set up a theme
- Show the flaw
- End with a question the story will answer

**Teaser Act-Out**: The moment that hooks us.

### Act 1 — Setup of Trigger, Dilemma, and Goal
- Set up the Pilot Trigger and Dilemma
- Introduce key characters
- Set up the conflict
- By the END of this act: **TOTAL CLARITY on the A story goal**
- The B story goal is often established here too
- End on the A story — either the clear setup of the goal or the first obstacle to it

**CRITICAL**: If the goal is not clear by the end of Act 1, the rest of the story cannot build effectively.

**Act 1 Act-Out (Break into 2)**: First obstacle or clear goal establishment.

### Act 2 — Obstacles and Escalation
- Central character actively pursues the goal
- Escalate the conflict with obstacles in A and B stories
- Deepen the stakes
- End on an obstacle or escalating obstacle that directly gets in the way of the goal

**Act 2 Act-Out (Midpoint)**: Obstacle or escalating obstacle to the goal.

### Act 3 — Escalation to "All Is Lost"
- When the character overcomes one obstacle, present another — escalate
- Build toward the "all is lost" moment
- The character should be further from the goal than ever

**Act 3 Act-Out (All Is Lost)**: The character hits their lowest point. This is where VOICE lives — what the character is truly made of is revealed here.

### Act 4 — Resolution
- Central character ACTIVELY solves the goal set up in Act 1 — in BOTH the A and B stories
- We need to see and feel the moment of achievement
- Set up the long-term stakes that will bring the audience back next week
- Show growth between where the character started and where they end

**Act 4 Act-Out (End of Pilot)**: Resolution plus setup for the series ahead. The concept of the series should be crystal clear by this point.

## Episodic Structure: Teaser + 4 Acts (Episodes 2+)

This is the standard format for all episodes after the pilot. Episodic storylines should be self-contained while also connecting to overarching series arcs.

**Key differences from the pilot**: Character backstories and world-building elements established in the pilot can be referenced or built upon, but NOT repeated in full. Episodes focus on introducing NEW conflicts, challenges, and character dynamics that expand and deepen the series narrative. Themes and emotional stakes should resonate with those established in the pilot, but be explored in new and unexpected ways.

### Teaser (5-7 minutes)
- Introduce a new dilemma or challenge that ties into ongoing storylines and character arcs
- Create a sense of urgency or stakes that will drive the action of the episode
- Highlight a specific character or relationship that will be the focus of the episode

### Act 1 (10-12 minutes)
- Establish the main storyline (A story) for the episode, building on the dilemma introduced in the teaser
- Introduce subplots (B and C stories) that complement or contrast with the main storyline
- Deepen character motivations and conflict, tying them to the episode's central theme

**Act 1 Act-Out (Break into 2)**: End with a turning point or complication that raises the stakes and propels the story forward.

### Act 2 (10-12 minutes)
- Escalate the main conflict and introduce new obstacles or challenges for the characters
- Develop the subplots, revealing new information or shifting character dynamics
- Explore the emotional and psychological impact of the events on the characters

**Act 2 Act-Out (Midpoint)**: End with a revelation, twist, or cliffhanger that intensifies the conflict and sets up the second half of the episode.

### Act 3 (10-12 minutes)
- Raise the tension and stakes, forcing characters to confront their fears or make difficult decisions
- Bring the main storyline and subplots to a head, creating urgency and anticipation
- Offer moments of emotional depth or character insight that resonate with the series' themes

**Act 3 Act-Out (All Is Lost)**: End with a major setback or reversal that leaves the characters at their lowest point.

### Act 4 (10-12 minutes)
- Resolve the main conflict and subplots, tying them together thematically and emotionally
- Provide a sense of resolution or transformation for the characters while hinting at ongoing challenges or unresolved issues
- Set up new storylines or character dynamics that will carry forward into future episodes

**Act 4 Act-Out (Episode Climax and Cliffhanger)**: End with a final scene or montage that underscores the central theme and leaves the audience eager for more.

## Half-Hour Comedy: 2-Act Structure

Key difference: Usually TWO goals (one per act).

### Act I
- Set up first goal
- Show first obstacle
- Show action taken to overcome obstacle
- End in jeopardy

### Act II
- Escalate jeopardy
- Set a SECOND goal
- Character goes into action to solve dilemma
- It gets worse
- Twist
- Resolution

Begin and end acts with A story. B and C stories have beginning, middle, end. Tie them together thematically.

---

## Half-Hour Comedy: 3-Act Structure (Drama-Style)

More like dramas — ONE goal per story rather than forming new goal after first act.

### Cold Open
- Set up world and concept

### Act I
- Set up dilemma in A and B story
- Goals stem from dilemma by end of act
- End on: obstacle to goal in A story

### Act II
- Escalate obstacle in A story
- Show obstacles in A and B stories
- Possible C runner
- End on: A or B story in jeopardy

### Act III
- Hit another obstacle
- Resolutions in each story
- End on: A story resolution

### Tag
- Expand on element from any story
- Can start with A story resolution, end on C runner beat

---

## Half-Hour Cable (No Act Breaks)

Mix between one-hour and half-hour format, structured more like one-hour. Usually A, B, and C story.

Structure without breaks:
1. Set up of dilemma
2. Goal stemming from dilemma
3. Obstacle to goal
4. Escalating obstacle to goal
5. Turning point
6. Resolution

B and C may not hit all beats but must have beginning, middle, end.

## Scene Checklist

Every scene must answer YES to these:

- Does the scene have a beginning, middle, and end?
- Does the character have a goal in this scene?
- Is there conflict or a point of tension?
- Does the scene move the plot forward?
- Does the scene cause change (minor or significant)?
- Is the character in a different emotional place at the end than the start?
- Does the last line create anticipation for what's next?

**Scene Arc**: Start positive/end negative OR start negative/end positive. Start comic/end dramatic. There should be a difference between how you start and finish.

**Scene-Level Wound/Flaw/Desire Check**:
- **Wound (WHY)**: How does the character's past trauma drive their actions in this scene?
- **Flaw (CONFLICT)**: How do the character's self-defeating behaviors create tension?
- **Desire (WANT)**: What does the character want to achieve in this scene? How does it tie to the overall goal?

## Pilot-Specific Checklist

### Story Concept
- Is there a strong engine for endless story?
- Is the world/franchise clear?
- What will episode #100 look like?
- Does the concept have a unique hook?

### Characters
- Is the central character complex, flawed, vulnerable?
- Do we empathize with them?
- Are they ACTIVE in pursuing their goal?
- Do supporting characters have distinct voices?
- What are their character arcs for Season 1? Season 2?

### Structure
- Is the dilemma clear in the teaser?
- Is the goal clear by end of Act I?
- Do act outs reflect back to the goal?
- Are obstacles escalating?
- Is there a clear "all is lost" moment?
- Does the central character actively solve the goal?

### Series Setup
- Does the pilot show what the series will be (not just backstory)?
- Is there enough story to suggest endless possibilities?
- Can I imagine Season 4?

**Red flag**: If backstory dominates Acts I-III and the series only starts in Act IV, restructure. "Move Act IV to Act I."

## Page Count Guidelines

### One-Hour Drama (Teaser + 4 Acts)
- Total: 55-65 pages
- Teaser: 5-10 pages
- Acts: 10-15 pages each

### Half-Hour Comedy
- Total: 28-35 pages
- Acts distributed based on structure

Study produced episodes of similar shows to get exact page counts per act.
