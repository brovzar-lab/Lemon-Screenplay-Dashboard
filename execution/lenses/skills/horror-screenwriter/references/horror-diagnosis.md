# Horror Screenplay Diagnosis

Comprehensive checklists for analyzing and improving existing horror screenplays.

---

## FIRST PASS: Gut Reaction

Read the entire script once without stopping. Then answer:

### Emotional Response
- [ ] Where did I feel genuine dread/fear/unease? (Note page numbers)
- [ ] Where did my attention wander? (Note page numbers)
- [ ] Did any scare actually make me tense up?
- [ ] What image/moment lingers after reading?
- [ ] Did the ending land emotionally?

### Instinct Check
- [ ] Do I care about the protagonist?
- [ ] Is the monster memorable?
- [ ] Would I recommend this to a horror fan?
- [ ] What's the ONE thing that doesn't work?
- [ ] What's the ONE thing that absolutely works?

---

## STRUCTURAL DIAGNOSIS

### The Ghost (Sins of the Past)
**Question:** What past event drives this story?

- [ ] Is there a clear Ghost (sin from the past)?
- [ ] Does the Ghost connect to the hero's psychological weakness?
- [ ] Does the Ghost connect to the hero's moral weakness?
- [ ] Does the Ghost keep attacking throughout (not just setup)?
- [ ] Is the Ghost the reason the monster attacks THIS person?

**Red Flag:** If you can't identify the Ghost, the horror has no psychological engine.

**Fix:** Create structural alignment: Past crime → Present flaw → Future horror

---

### The Monster

**Question:** Is this monster unique and properly deployed?

- [ ] Is the monster distinct from other horror movie monsters?
- [ ] Does the monster have clear, consistent rules?
- [ ] Is there a weakness the hero can exploit?
- [ ] Is revelation properly staged (not given away too early)?
- [ ] Does the monster represent something about the hero (the Double)?
- [ ] Does the monster embody the theme?

**Red Flag:** Monster feels generic or has no connection to hero's psychology.

**Fix:** Strengthen the Double relationship. Monster should be hero's flaw externalized.

---

### The Haunted House (Setting)

**Question:** Does the setting express the hero's psychology?

- [ ] Is the setting specific (not generic horror location)?
- [ ] Does it reflect the hero's Ghost and weakness?
- [ ] Does it isolate/trap the characters?
- [ ] Does it have its own character/presence?
- [ ] Are there meaningful spaces (cellar = buried sins, attic = locked memories)?

**Red Flag:** Setting feels interchangeable. Could be any haunted house/cabin/hospital.

**Fix:** Make setting an expression of hero's internal state. Location IS character.

---

### Hero/Victim

**Question:** Do we care before the horror starts?

- [ ] Is the hero intensely human in the opening?
- [ ] Do we understand their life BEFORE horror invades?
- [ ] Are they active (making choices) or purely reactive?
- [ ] Do they have a clear psychological weakness?
- [ ] Do they have a clear moral weakness?
- [ ] Even if they die/fail, is there an arc?

**Red Flag:** Hero is just "person things happen to."

**Fix:** Spend time making them human first. Give them specific desires, flaws, relationships.

---

### Pressure and Escalation

**Question:** Does each sequence increase the stakes?

- [ ] Does each monster encounter escalate from the previous?
- [ ] Is there variety in attack types (not same beat repeated)?
- [ ] Does the pacing accelerate toward the climax (cyclone effect)?
- [ ] Are there valleys between peaks (rest before next scare)?
- [ ] Does the final battle feel like the culmination?

**Red Flag:** Same intensity throughout. Repetitive attack pattern.

**Fix:** Map escalation. Each nightmare must be worse than the last.

---

### Scares

**Question:** Do the scares actually work?

- [ ] Is each scare sequence properly structured (expectation → tension → misdirection → payoff)?
- [ ] Are there different types of scares (jump, dread, reveal, slow burn)?
- [ ] Are jump scares earned (tension built first)?
- [ ] Are there character reactions after scares (mirroring effect)?
- [ ] Do scares advance the story or just punctuate it?

**Red Flag:** Scares feel mechanical or don't land emotionally.

**Fix:** Vary scare types. Build tension before release. Show reactions.

---

## BEAT-BY-BEAT CHECKLIST

### Act One (Setup)
- [ ] Ghost established (even if not fully revealed)
- [ ] Hero's normal world shown
- [ ] Hero's weakness visible
- [ ] Haunted house/setting introduced
- [ ] First sign of monster (or wrongness)
- [ ] Hero drawn into horror (not seeking it)
- [ ] Stakes established (what can be lost)

### Act Two (Confrontation)
- [ ] Monster attacks (puts hero on defensive)
- [ ] Hero's weakness exploited
- [ ] Attempts to escape/defeat fail
- [ ] Rules of monster become clearer
- [ ] Pressure escalates continuously
- [ ] Allies tested (some may die)
- [ ] All hope lost moment (apparent defeat)

### Act Three (Resolution)
- [ ] Hero formulates final plan
- [ ] Cyclone effect (rapid escalation)
- [ ] Direct confrontation with monster
- [ ] Hero's weakness either overcome or their undoing
- [ ] Moral decision moment
- [ ] Monster defeated/escaped/wins
- [ ] New equilibrium (world changed)

## TRANSCENDENCE CHECK

For horror that aspires to more than genre exercise:

### Thematic Depth
- [ ] Does the horror explore a genuine human fear (not just "monster attacks")?
- [ ] Does the monster represent something about humanity/society?
- [ ] Does confronting the monster require confronting the self?
- [ ] Are there social/political/universal ramifications?
- [ ] Does the story ask "what is human"?

### The Structural Flip (if applicable)
- [ ] Who appears to be the monster at the start?
- [ ] Is there a moment where apparent monster reveals humanity?
- [ ] Is there a moment where apparent humans reveal monstrousness?
- [ ] Does the flip force audience to question their assumptions?

### Earning Transcendence
- [ ] Are basic genre beats executed well first?
- [ ] Is the theme organic to the premise (not tacked on)?
- [ ] Does transcendence serve the horror (not replace it)?

**Red Flag:** Trying for social commentary without earning basic scares.

**Fix:** Master the genre first. Transcendence builds on solid foundation.

## COMMON PROBLEMS & FIXES

### Problem: "Nothing happens for 30 pages"
**Diagnosis:** Slow burn without buildup; mistaking boring for atmospheric.
**Fix:** Wrongness should accumulate from page one. Atmosphere isn't absence of story.

### Problem: "I stopped caring about the protagonist"
**Diagnosis:** Not enough humanity before horror; or hero too passive.
**Fix:** Make them specific, relatable, and active. Give them choices.

### Problem: "The monster isn't scary"
**Diagnosis:** Over-revealed, under-developed, or no connection to hero.
**Fix:** Stage revelation. Build mystery. Connect to hero's psychology.

### Problem: "The ending doesn't work"
**Diagnosis:** Not earned; doesn't resolve the Ghost; or gratuitously bleak.
**Fix:** Ending must connect to beginning. Ghost must be addressed (even if negatively).

### Problem: "It feels like every other horror movie"
**Diagnosis:** Relying on genre conventions without fresh perspective.
**Fix:** Find the unique angle. What does YOUR version say that others don't?

### Problem: "The scares are predictable"
**Diagnosis:** Following obvious patterns; not using misdirection.
**Fix:** Subvert expectations. Use genre knowledge against the audience.

### Problem: "There's no theme"
**Diagnosis:** Horror without meaning; just a monster attacking people.
**Fix:** Ask: What human fear does this explore? What does the monster represent?

## PRIORITY SYSTEM FOR NOTES

### Critical (Script doesn't work without fixing)
- No Ghost/structural alignment
- Protagonist we don't care about
- Monster has no rules or connection to hero
- No escalation/pressure
- Ending doesn't land

### Major (Significantly weakens impact)
- Repetitive scares
- Passive hero
- Generic setting
- Unearned revelations
- Pacing problems

### Polish (Elevates good to great)
- Strengthen specific scares
- Deepen atmospheric details
- Refine dialogue
- Tighten individual sequences
- Add subtext layers

---

## FINAL QUESTIONS

Before declaring analysis complete:

1. What is this horror movie ABOUT (not plot—theme)?
2. Why does this story need to be told as horror?
3. What will audiences remember a week later?
4. What does this add to the horror genre?
5. Would you watch this movie?
