# Lemon Comedy Engine

## Purpose

Generate, evaluate, and refine high-concept comedy movie ideas calibrated for Mexican theatrical audiences with global crossover potential. The engine is built on the DNA of proven Mexican box-office hits: *Nosotros los Nobles*, *Matando Cabos*, *Salvando al Soldado Pérez*. It runs across comedy registers (satirical, absurd, cringe, and heartfelt) and treats the commercial title and word-of-mouth pull as first-class filters, not afterthoughts.

## Rules

- **High concept is the #1 filter, and the real test is word of mouth.** The premise has to be funny and clear enough that someone hears the title or the one-line and immediately wants to text a friend 'tienes que ver esta.' If a concept needs explaining, it won't travel by word of mouth, and word of mouth is how comedy wins theaters in Mexico. Concepts that fail this don't get killed on sight; they go back to REFINE until the title alone earns a laugh. Pressure-test commercial titles with the **titulo-comercial** skill.
- **Mexican specificity drives universality.** The more authentically rooted in Mexican reality, the more universally the comedy resonates. This is the Coco Principle applied to comedy.
- **Funny is non-negotiable. A single tone is not.** The engine runs across registers: hard satirical LOL, cringe-truth recognition, heightened absurdity, and earned emotional comedy (the *No se Aceptan Devoluciones* lane, where the laugh and the gut-punch come from the same moment). What can't move is that the concept is genuinely, repeatedly funny. Match the register to what the premise actually wants instead of forcing teeth onto a story built for heart, or sentiment onto a story built for teeth. See **Comedy Registers & Specialist Handoffs** below for which specialist to pull for each.
- **Fish-out-of-water is the default structure.** One protagonist catapulted into an absurd world they are completely unequipped for. The premise IS the displacement.
- **Bilingual output.** Loglines and concept pitches in SPANISH (they must feel authentically Mexican). Scoring and analysis in ENGLISH.
- **Theatrical-first, streaming-aware.** Default assumption is a theatrical release. Flag concepts that might play better as streaming originals (Netflix, Prime, ViX).
- **Soft guardrails only.** Flag potentially sensitive territory (narco glorification, harmful stereotypes, poverty porn) but NEVER kill an idea. The human decides.

## Overview

Three operating modes that work independently or as a pipeline:

```
BRAINSTORM → EVALUATE → REFINE
```

Activate the appropriate mode based on user intent, or run the full pipeline when asked for end-to-end development.

## Comedy Registers & Specialist Handoffs

The engine is one filter, not one tone. Once you know which register a concept lives in, pull the specialist skill that goes deepest on it. The engine sets up the premise; the specialist takes it further on voice, escalation, or feeling.

| Register | What it feels like | Mexican benchmark | Pull this skill |
|----------|--------------------|--------------------|-----------------|
| **Satirical / system takedown** | Big, smart-funny about power: corruption, narco-money, bureaucracy, institutions | *La Ley de Herodes*, *Salvando al Soldado Pérez* | **adam-mckay**: heighten the game, make the corrupt system legible and absurd |
| **Heightened absurd** | Reality pushed to its dumbest logical conclusion; commit to the bit | *Matando Cabos* | **adam-mckay**: escalation, the scene's game, full commitment |
| **Heartfelt / cry-comedy** | The laugh and the gut-punch land in the same scene | *No se Aceptan Devoluciones*, *¿Qué Culpa Tiene el Niño?* | **judd-apatow**: find the pain under the joke, earn the feeling |
| **Cringe / social friction** | Recognition humor; archetypes forced into proximity | *Mirreyes vs Godínez*, *Nosotros los Nobles* | **judd-apatow**: character depth so the cringe has a real person underneath |

Rules of thumb:
- A concept can blend registers, and most hits do. Pick the **dominant** one to decide the handoff, then let the specialist find the others.
- McKay's value is loudest when the comedy is about a **system** the audience has to understand in order to laugh (money, cartels, corruption, institutional rot). If that's the engine, hand off early.
- Apatow's value is loudest when there's a **drama hiding inside the comedy** and the feeling has to be earned, not stated.
- After a concept greenlights, the next stop is **co-writer** for pages, and **titulo-comercial** to lock the commercial title if it isn't locked already.

## Mode 1: BRAINSTORM

Generate 10-15 comedy concepts using five brainstorming engines. Lead with the title: if the title doesn't land, the concept underneath usually won't travel either. For each concept, output:

1. **Commercial Title (ES):** the funny, instantly-clear title that does the word-of-mouth work on its own
2. **Logline (ES):** one sentence, high concept, immediately funny
3. **Setup (ES):** 3-5 sentences covering world, protagonist, central conflict, comedic engine
4. **Comp Line:** "It's [X] meets [Y]" using known titles
5. **Primary Pain Point:** which Mexican cultural tension powers the comedy
6. **Register:** satirical, absurd, heartfelt, or cringe (this sets the specialist handoff)

Run your finalists through the **titulo-comercial** skill to sharpen the titles before moving to EVALUATE.

### The Five Brainstorming Engines

Run ALL five engines and generate 2-3 concepts from each:

#### Engine 1: Comp-Based Generation
Take a successful international comedy and reimagine it through a Mexican lens.

Reference pool (not exhaustive):
- *The Hangover* → Mexican bachelor party chaos
- *Bridesmaids* → Mexican wedding dynamics
- *Game Night* → Mexican family game escalation
- *Knives Out* → Mexican inheritance war
- *The Grand Budapest Hotel* → Mexican institutional absurdity
- *Parasite* → Mexican class infiltration (darker comedy)
- *Borat* → Mexican fish-out-of-water in foreign territory

**Key rule:** The adaptation must find the MEXICAN version of the underlying tension, not just transplant the plot.

#### Engine 2: Current Events Scan
Identify what Mexico is talking about RIGHT NOW (political absurdity, social media controversies, cultural flashpoints, economic pressures, generational tensions) and build comedy concepts around them.

Prioritize topics with high emotional charge where comedy provides catharsis.

#### Engine 3: Pain Point Deep Dive
Select one of the five core pain points and generate concepts that exploit it specifically:

| Pain Point | Comedy Vein | Example Tension |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Classism** | The insane gap between rich and poor, mirreyes vs godínez, fresa culture, aspirational delusion | A godínez accidentally becomes a mirrey's life coach |
| **Family** | The suffocating but loving Mexican family, metiches, the suegra, the family WhatsApp group from hell | A man tries to have one secret from his family of 47 |
| **Corruption/Bureaucracy** | The mordida, the trámite, the Kafka-esque system everyone navigates daily | Someone tries to do ONE thing 100% legally in Mexico |
| **Social Climbing** | The faking-it culture, keeping up appearances, the préstame-para-la-foto mentality | A family pretends to be rich for an entire vacation |
| **Machismo** | Toxic masculinity, the fragile macho ego, gender role absurdity | A macho man must survive a situation that requires stereotypically "feminine" skills |

**Core insight:** The comedy vein in ALL five pain points is the same: **the gap between who Mexicans ARE and who they PRETEND to be.**

#### Engine 4: What-If Generator
Pure high-concept speculation rooted in Mexican reality.

Format: "¿Qué pasaría si...?" [absurd but emotionally true premise]

The best What-Ifs take something every Mexican has experienced and push it to its most absurd logical conclusion.

Examples of the LEVEL of absurdity to target:
- What if the most corrupt politician in Mexico suddenly couldn't lie?
- What if a family's WhatsApp group messages became public?
- What if you had to survive a week using only official government processes?

#### Engine 5: Genre Mashup
Take a proven genre template and inject Mexican comedy DNA:

| Genre | Mexican Comedy Twist |
|-------|---------------------|
| Heist | Stealing from the system that steals from you |
| Thriller/Suspense | The danger is bureaucratic or familial, not criminal |
| Sports | An underdog in a sport Mexico doesn't care about |
| Workplace | The absurdity of Mexican office culture / corporate-meets-informal |
| Road Trip | A journey through Mexico's insane regional diversity |
| Survival | Surviving a Mexican institution (hospital, government office, family reunion) |

## Mode 2: EVALUATE

Score any comedy concept (user-provided or generated) against the **Lemon Comedy Rubric**.

### The Lemon Comedy Rubric

#### Tier 1: MUST-HAVE (50% of total score)

| Criterion | Weight | Score 1-10 | What a 10 looks like |
|-----------|--------|-----------|----------------------|
| **High Concept Clarity** | 25% | _ | One sentence. Instant understanding. Instant laugh. *Nosotros los Nobles* level: "Billionaire dad forces his spoiled kids to live as poor people." |
| **Laugh Density Potential** | 25% | _ | The concept is a comedy MACHINE where every scene naturally generates conflict and absurdity. You can immediately imagine 20+ funny scenes. |

#### Tier 2: SHOULD-HAVE (35% of total score)

| Criterion | Weight | Score 1-10 | What a 10 looks like |
|-----------|--------|-----------|----------------------|
| **Global Travel Potential** | 12% | _ | Audiences in Spain, Brazil, US, France, Germany would GET this and laugh. The emotions are universal even if the details are Mexican. |
| **Cultural Pain Point Depth** | 12% | _ | Hits a REAL nerve. Makes Mexicans say "güey, that's SO true." The laughter comes from recognition, not just jokes. |
| **Zeitgeist Relevance** | 11% | _ | Taps into what Mexico is feeling RIGHT NOW. The concept feels urgent, timely, of-the-moment. |

#### Tier 3: NICE-TO-HAVE (15% of total score)

| Criterion | Weight | Score 1-10 | What a 10 looks like |
|-----------|--------|-----------|----------------------|
| **Fish-Out-of-Water Displacement** | 8% | _ | The protagonist's displacement is extreme, visual, and endlessly generative. Every scene is a new collision between who they are and where they are. |
| **Franchise Potential** | 7% | _ | The concept naturally supports sequels, spin-offs, or can become a comedy brand. The world is bigger than one story. |

### Scoring Thresholds

| Composite Score | Verdict | Action |
|----------------|---------|--------|
| **85-100** | 🟢 **GREEN LIGHT.** This is a hit. Develop immediately. | Fast-track to treatment/script |
| **70-84** | 🟡 **STRONG PROSPECT.** Great bones, needs refinement. | Move to REFINE mode |
| **55-69** | 🟠 **HAS POTENTIAL.** Interesting kernel, but major gaps. | Identify weak spots, rethink |
| **40-54** | 🔴 **PASS.** Concept doesn't have enough commercial fuel. | Salvage any strong elements for new concepts |
| **Below 40** | ⚫ **DEAD.** Fundamental concept problem. | Start over |

### Kill Gates (Hard Filters)

Before scoring, run these pass/fail checks:

1. **The Bar Napkin Test:** Can you explain this concept to a stranger at a bar and they laugh? If NO → ⚫ DEAD.
2. **The "So What" Test:** Does this concept NEED to exist as a movie, or is it just a sketch/skit? If it's a skit → 🔴 PASS.
3. **The Cringe Check:** Is the comedy punching DOWN at vulnerable people, or punching UP/SIDEWAYS at systems, power, and absurdity? If punching down → ⚠️ FLAG (soft guardrail).
4. **The Word-of-Mouth Test:** Does the title plus one line make someone want to tell a friend, unprompted? If they'd have to explain it first, the concept isn't traveling yet → 🟠 back to REFINE to sharpen the title and the hook before scoring.

## Mode 3: REFINE

Take a concept scoring 55+ and elevate it. The refine process:

### Step 1: Identify the Weak Axis
Which scoring criteria pulled the composite down? Focus there.

### Step 2: Apply Comedy Surgery

| Weak Criterion | Surgery |
|---------------|---------|
| High Concept unclear | Simplify. Strip to ONE ironic reversal. Remove complexity. |
| Low Laugh Density | Add more COLLISION POINTS, situations where the protagonist's identity clashes with their environment. |
| Weak Global Travel | Find the universal emotion underneath. Poverty, family shame, imposter syndrome all travel everywhere. |
| Shallow Pain Point | Go DEEPER into the specific Mexican version. Add details only someone who lived it would know. |
| Low Zeitgeist | Connect to a current cultural conversation. What headline could this movie respond to? |
| Weak Fish-Out-of-Water | Increase the DISTANCE between protagonist and situation. The bigger the gap, the funnier. |
| No Franchise | Build the WORLD, not just the plot. Are there other characters whose stories you want to see? |

**Specialist surgery.** If the weak axis is emotional truth or character (the comedy isn't landing because there's no real person underneath it), hand the concept to **judd-apatow** for a pass. If the weak axis is scale, escalation, or a system that isn't legible enough to be funny (money, cartels, corruption, institutions), hand it to **adam-mckay**. They do the deep pass; bring the result back here to rescore.

### Step 3: Rewrite and Rescore
Output the refined logline + setup in Spanish, then re-score against the rubric. Show the DELTA (before → after) for each criterion.

### Step 4: The Killer Scene Test
Describe the ONE scene from this movie that would go viral, the scene people would talk about leaving the theater. If you can't imagine that scene, the concept still needs work. Run that scene against the Viral Scene Checklist in `references/detailed-guide.md` (quotable, physical, escalating, recognizable, rewatchable): a scene that clears all five is your word-of-mouth engine.

## Reference Titles (Calibration Benchmarks)

These films define the quality bar. Any concept generated or evaluated should be measured against this standard:

| Film | Why It Works | Key Lesson |
|------|-------------|------------|
| *Nosotros los Nobles* (2013) | Perfect high concept + class satire + ensemble laughs | Class reversal = comedy gold |
| *Matando Cabos* (2004) | Genre mashup (crime + comedy) + dark humor + ensemble chaos | Multiple storylines colliding = escalating absurdity |
| *Salvando al Soldado Pérez* (2011) | Genre mashup (war film + narco comedy) + family loyalty | Taking a Hollywood genre and making it absurdly Mexican |
| *No se Aceptan Devoluciones* (2013) | Fish-out-of-water + emotional gut punch + Derbez star power | Comedy that makes you cry hits HARDER |
| *Mirreyes vs Godínez* (2019) | Class collision + workplace comedy + archetypes everyone recognizes | Social archetypes in forced proximity = friction = laughs |
| *¿Qué Culpa Tiene el Niño?* (2016) | High concept (surprise baby) + class comedy + romantic element | Simple premise, maximum complications |

## Anti-Patterns (Soft Guardrails)

Flag but do NOT kill ideas that contain:

- ⚠️ **Narco glorification:** cartel life as aspirational or cool (vs satirizing it, which IS fine)
- ⚠️ **Poverty porn:** poor people as punchlines rather than protagonists
- ⚠️ **Cringe stereotypes:** lazy "all Mexicans are..." humor that wouldn't survive Twitter/X
- ⚠️ **Dated references:** humor that relies on 2010s cultural references
- ⚠️ **Gringo gaze:** concepts that feel like a foreigner's idea of Mexico
- ⚠️ **Solo sketch:** a concept that's really a 5-minute SNL sketch stretched to 90 minutes
