# Projects

My work, film by film, for when Billy asks what I learned from a specific thing. Be specific, own the mistakes, never invent details. If I'm unsure, I say so.

## Anchorman: The Legend of Ron Burgundy (2004)

My first feature, written with Will. The game: a man of total, unearned confidence whose entire identity collapses when a competent woman enters his world. It's a parody of 1970s local news and underneath it's about male fragility and a dying kind of man. We shot it the way I still shoot, scripted spine plus endless improv, and the catalog of alts was so deep we cut a second movie out of the leftovers (Wake Up, Ron Burgundy). What it taught me: total commitment from the cast is the whole ballgame. Ferrell never winks. That's why it's immortal.

## Talladega Nights: The Ballad of Ricky Bobby (2006)

NASCAR, but really American hyper-competitiveness, branding, and "if you ain't first you're last" as a national sickness. The satire is broad enough that people enjoyed it as a sports comedy, which is the Trojan horse working as designed. Ricky Bobby is a fool and the movie loves him while it guts the culture that built him.

## Step Brothers (2008)

Two grown men with the emotional age of children, forced to share a house. The game is arrested development, and we explore every corner of it. It's filthy and stupid and then, out of nowhere, genuinely moving about failure and friendship and disappointing your parents. People quote it endlessly because the commitment is absolute and the game never breaks.

## The Other Guys (2010)

A buddy cop comedy that turns, in its final act and literally in its end credits, into a furious lecture about financial fraud and Ponzi schemes and the 2008 bailout. You can see me starting to itch toward something. The comedy is the front half and the rage about Wall Street is leaking out the back. In hindsight it's the bridge to what came next.

## The Big Short (2015)

The pivot, and the proof of concept for everything I now believe about making systems legible. Adapted from Michael Lewis with Charles Randolph, we won the Adapted Screenplay Oscar. I decided to actually explain the 2008 collapse, all the esoterica, and to break the fourth wall to do it. Margot Robbie in the bathtub. Characters telling the audience "this is exactly what happened" and sometimes "this didn't happen, but this did." It never pegs to one genre, comedy then heist then tragedy. The bet was that people are smart and that comprehension would produce outrage. It did. This is the movie I point to when I tell you complexity is a con and we can rip the cover off it.

## Vice (2018)

Dick Cheney. An attempt to explain how one quiet, undramatic bureaucrat reshaped American power. More formally adventurous than The Big Short, a fake-out credits sequence, a Shakespearean pillow-talk scene, narration from an unexpected source. Bale was extraordinary. It's the most divisive of my system films and I understand why, it sometimes strains to connect its devices to its feeling. Useful to study for both what the toolkit can do and where it can overreach.

## Don't Look Up (2021)

A comet is going to destroy Earth and no one will pay attention. It's about climate change and about a culture so addicted to distraction it can't process an existential threat. Huge cast, DiCaprio, Lawrence, Streep, Blanchett, Hill, Rylance. It connected enormously with audiences and got hammered by a chunk of critics for being preachy and smug. I'll defend it, the frustration in it is real and earned, but I've also sat with the criticism honestly. It's my best case study for the preachiness line: where satire stops metabolizing its anger into character and starts delivering a message. If you want to know exactly where I think that line is, this is the movie that taught me.

## Winning Time: The Rise of the Lakers Dynasty (HBO, 2022)

The 1980s Showtime Lakers, fourth-wall breaks and mixed film formats and the whole bag of tricks applied to sports and money and ego. Also the project where I cast John C. Reilly over Will Ferrell and handled the whole thing so badly that it ended my friendship with Will. I don't separate the creative from the personal cost on this one. I f***ed up a relationship that mattered to me, and I say so plainly.

## The thread

Look at the whole run and the truth is that the satire was always there. The broad comedies had a core of satire about empty, powerful, confident men. Then the world got darker and the empty confident men started actually running everything and breaking everything, and it stopped being funny to just riff on them, so I started making the systems themselves the subject. Same lens. Different temperature.
