# Escalation

For ESCALATE mode, and for the fix list on any diagnosis where the complaint is "the stakes are already high and it still isn't building."

## First, decide what is actually flat

Three different problems get reported as "the stakes need to be higher."

**Static stakes.** The consequence of failure is identical on page 90 and page 10. The audience has already priced it in. Fix by escalation proper, below.

**Static cost.** The stakes grew but the hero is doing the same kind of thing all the way through, so nothing feels riskier. Fix by raising the cost of participation at the midpoint.

**Under-modulation.** Stakes and costs are both fine and the audience still does not feel it. This is the most common of the three and escalation will not fix it. Go to `modulators.md` and turn knobs instead. Adding another catastrophe on top of an unfelt catastrophe changes nothing.

Diagnose which one before prescribing.

## The two engines of escalation

Everything reduces to these.

**Raise the cost of participation.** The hero puts more skin in the game. Same stakes, worse consequences of failure, more tension. Cheapest and most reliable.

**Put new stakes into play.** Give the audience more to care about. Harder, and where the interesting work is.

## Where to place escalations

Two mandatory positions and one optional.

**The midpoint** is the primary one. This is the point of no return: options that existed in the first half of Act 2 are sealed off, and the hero fully commits. If the story looks the same after the midpoint as before, that is the finding.

**Immediately before the climax** is the second. This is where the atrocity lands, where the sacrifice is made, where the loved one is taken, where the clock tightens.

**Partway through the first half of Act 2** is optional and common in action and thriller, where an action-lite sequence delivers genre goods early without spending the real escalation.

## Techniques for adding new stakes

**Make it personal.** The reliable standby. Convert a general threat into a threat against someone the hero loves. Or let a person he was assigned to protect professionally become someone he cares about personally, which changes nothing external and everything internal. Cheap, effective, works in every genre.

**Make things worse by bending the world.** Change a rule of the story world so a new safety-need stake becomes possible. The penalty for failure inside the story's own system gets harsher, or a previously survivable outcome becomes permanent. Demands invention, pays the most when it works, and has the virtue of feeling like the story's own logic rather than an imposition.

**Pit one stake against another.** Instead of adding a threat, force a choice between two things the hero cannot both keep. This escalates without adding machinery and generates character revelation as a byproduct.

**Convert a risk into a stake.** Once the hero has crossed a line, he is no longer courting a consequence, he is avoiding one. Prison stops being reckless and becomes something he must now outrun. This is the standard repair for a story stuck in Cell 4.

**Foreshadow future stakes.** Hint that failing now means someone else pays later. Costs nothing structurally and adds a second current of worry underneath the first.

## Escalating a climax that already looks maxed out

When the hero is already risking his life to save someone he loves, the remaining moves are not bigger threats, they are deeper ones. In rough order of yield:

1. Tighten the clock and cut back to it.
2. Deplete a resource so the final attempt is the last one available.
3. Add a legacy dimension, so what he protects is also what he inherits.
4. Add regret, so winning also settles an old failure.
5. Extract a sacrifice just before the finale, so losing also wastes it.
6. Boost the likeability of the hero or of the stakes with one act of generosity at personal risk, kept inside the story's trajectory.
7. Cut away to the stakes during the climax itself, in images.

## The escalation traps

**Starting too hot.** A second act that opens with the boldest version of the plan has nowhere to go, and the second half will feel like a retread. The repair is to decide the maximum action, place it after the midpoint, and invent a diminished version for before it.

**Escalating cost past the stakes.** Adding danger without adding reason produces the misalignment failure: the audience stops fearing for the hero and starts questioning him. Check alignment every time cost goes up.

**Escalating the wrong direction at the end.** Going from a personal threat to a general one late reads as anticlimactic. General to personal works; personal to general does not, unless the general threat was foreshadowed early.

**Rescuing the stakes early in the climax.** The moment nothing is in danger, the climax is over and the resolution has begun, whatever the page count says. Retire one stake only by introducing another.

**Confusing escalation with volume.** More explosions, more shouting, and more corpses are not escalation if the consequence of failure has not changed. Escalation is measured in what is lost, not in what is destroyed.
