# Grisanti Pilot Analysis

Interactive analysis of TV pilot scripts using Jen Grisanti's methodology from her 15+ years as a studio executive at CBS/Paramount and Spelling Television, and as Writing Instructor for NBC's Writers on the Verge.

This skill is a **diagnostic tool**. It takes a written pilot script and works backwards — extracting what's there, identifying what's working and what's broken, and providing actionable notes. For developing a new series from an idea, use `grisanti-series-development`.

## Core Philosophy

One question drives everything: **What does your central character want?**

If this is unclear, the script cannot work. Everything flows from:
- A **TRIGGER INCIDENT** forces the character into a **DILEMMA**
- The choice made in the DILEMMA defines the **GOAL**
- Every act out reflects back to this GOAL
- **OBSTACLES** escalate until the **ALL IS LOST** moment
- The central character **ACTIVELY** achieves the goal in the resolution

If something in the script isn't working, the problem is usually upstream — an unclear trigger, a weak dilemma, or a muddy goal.

## Interactive Analysis Workflow

Conduct analysis as a conversation, not a document dump. Move through passes sequentially, discussing findings before proceeding. The writer should understand and agree with each diagnosis before moving forward.

### Pass 1: The Log Line Test

Extract or construct the pilot's log line using the formula:
- **WHO**: Set up empathy for central character
- **DILEMMA**: The impossible choice (neither option is good)
- **ACTION**: What they do as a result
- **GOAL**: What they want to achieve (often ironic — opposite of where they started)

Construct loglines at three levels:
- **Series Logline**: The big-picture logline for the entire series
- **Pilot Logline**: The A story of this episode
- **Internal Logline**: The protagonist's emotional journey

If the log line doesn't work, the story doesn't work. Diagnose here first.

**Ask the writer**: "Is this the story you're trying to tell?"

### Pass 2: Goal/Dilemma Clarity

The most critical assessment. Evaluate both the Series-level and Pilot-level:

**Series Trigger & Dilemma**:
- What event sets the entire series in motion?
- What impossible choice does it force? Can you articulate BOTH sides?
- Does it set up WHY the character wants WHAT they want?

**Pilot Trigger & Dilemma**:
- What specific event in Episode 1 forces the character to act?
- Is it distinct from the Series Trigger, or the same event?
- Does the pilot dilemma produce a clear goal by end of Act I?

**Pilot Pursuit**:
- What actions does the character take in response?
- Is the character ACTIVE or reactive?

**Diagnostic questions**:
- What is the worst that happens if they don't achieve the goal?
- Why is it so important to them internally (not just externally)?
- Can I feel the goal resonating in every scene?

If goal/dilemma is unclear, everything else falls apart. Stop and diagnose this before proceeding.

### Pass 3: Triangle of the Wound

Extract the character's psychological architecture:

**The Dream**: What does the character want most deeply?
**The Wound**: What past trauma shaped who they are?
**The Flaw**: What self-defeating behavior comes from the wound?
**The Biggest Fear**: What terrifies them? How does the series force them to confront it?

Map the **Triangle of the Wound**:
- THE WOUND (childhood trauma) → THE FLAW (self-sabotaging behavior) → THE REACTION (destructive response to inciting incident)

**Five Questions diagnostic** (see `references/assessment.md` for full detail):
1. Is the childhood wound identifiable?
2. Does the Series Trigger split open this wound?
3. Is there a negative narrative (the lie they tell themselves)?
4. Does the flaw visibly manifest in behavior?
5. Does the flaw get in the way of achieving the goal?

If the Triangle is underdeveloped, the character will feel thin regardless of how strong the plot is.

### Pass 4: Structural Mapping (Teaser + 4 Acts)

Map the script against the **Teaser + 4 Act** framework (see `references/structure.md` for full breakdown):

- **Teaser**: Does it set up the world, create empathy, establish the dilemma/trigger, show the flaw, end with a hook?
- **Act 1**: Is the Pilot Trigger and Dilemma established? Is the goal **crystal clear** by end of act? Are key characters introduced?
- **Act 2**: Is the character actively pursuing the goal? Are obstacles escalating? Does the midpoint raise stakes?
- **Act 3**: Does it build to the "All Is Lost" moment? Is the character furthest from the goal? Is voice emerging here?
- **Act 4**: Does the character ACTIVELY solve the goal? Is there growth? Are series stakes set up?

For each act out, answer:
- Does this reflect back to the initial goal?
- Is it an obstacle, escalating obstacle, turning point, or "all is lost" moment?
- Does it create anticipation for what's next?

**Common structural problems**:
- Too much backstory before the series starts ("Move Act IV to Act I")
- Act outs that don't connect to the goal
- Obstacles that don't escalate
- Central character who is reactive rather than active
- Goal not clear by end of Act 1

### Pass 5: Emotional Stakes Assessment

**The 20-Minute Test**: Do I know why I should care within 20 minutes? If not, the audience changes the channel.

Evaluate:
- What's at stake externally? (What they want)
- What's at stake internally? (Why they want it)
- Have both been clearly established?

**Stakes Arc** — map escalation through the pilot:
- Teaser/Act 1 Stakes: Initial establishment
- Act 2 Stakes: Escalation
- Act 3 Stakes: Highest point (All Is Lost)
- Act 4 Stakes: Resolution plus new stakes for series

### Pass 6: A/B/C Story Integration

Each story needs: Trigger → Dilemma → Goal → Obstacles → Escalation → "All is Lost" → Resolution.

Check:
- Does the B story elevate the A story thematically?
- Does each story have its own trigger, dilemma, and goal?
- Is there a clear balance of professional to personal?
- Do acts begin and end on the A story?
- Does every scene move plot forward (not just character development or backstory)?
- What single theme ties all storylines together?

### Pass 7: Series Potential & Engine

A pilot must answer: **Can I see Season 4?**

Evaluate the engine — the mechanism that generates new stories:
- **Franchise engine**: Does the world generate stories?
- **Character engine**: Does the wound/flaw create recurring conflict?
- **Relationship engine**: Do central relationships generate tension?
- **Mystery engine**: Does an overarching mystery sustain?

Check:
- Is the world/franchise clear?
- Does the pilot give a sense of what the series will be?
- What does a typical episode look like after the pilot?
- Did the writer wait too long to show us what the series will be?

**Red flag**: If the series only starts in the last act, the pilot spent too much time on backstory.

### Pass 8: Episodic Viability

Using the **Episodic Teaser + 4 Act** structure for Episodes 2+ (see `references/structure.md`), assess whether the pilot sets up viable episodic storytelling:

- Can self-contained episodic storylines emerge from this world?
- Are there ongoing arcs that can be referenced without repeating backstory?
- Does the pilot introduce enough character dynamics to fuel new conflicts?
- Can themes be explored in new and unexpected ways episode to episode?

### Pass 9: Voice Opportunities

Voice is the gold. It separates your script from the hundreds on the executive's desk.

Identify moments where the writer could go deeper:
- The "all is lost" moment (Acts 3-4) — best place to reveal what character is made of
- Moments of extreme emotion — what would the character NEVER say out loud?
- Universal themes drawn from the writer's own emotional well

**Ask**: Where in this script do I hear the writer's truth fictionalized?

## Giving Notes

Balance executive perspective (fix this) with instructional perspective (here's why).

**Structure notes as**:
1. What's working and why
2. What's not working and why
3. Specific actionable fixes
4. Questions for the writer to consider

All notes should help clarify or strengthen the central character's journey from wound through dilemma to goal.

## Quick Diagnostics

**If the script feels flat**: Check if the goal is clear and if the central character is active (not reactive) in pursuing it.

**If the script feels confusing**: Check if the dilemma was properly established and if act outs reflect back to the goal. Check if both sides of the dilemma are clear.

**If the script feels generic**: Check for voice — where is the writer's truth fictionalized? Check the Triangle of the Wound — is it fully developed?

**If the pilot doesn't feel like a series**: Check if the world has an engine for endless story and if the series starts early enough (not just in the last act).

**If the character feels thin**: Check the Triangle of the Wound. Is the wound clear? Does the flaw visibly manifest? Does the fear connect to the series stakes?

**If something feels off but you can't pinpoint it**: The problem is usually upstream. Go back to the trigger, dilemma, and goal.
