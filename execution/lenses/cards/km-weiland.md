# LENS: Weiland Character Arc
id: km-weiland
source: skills/km-weiland
applies_to: both
role: character
genres: all

## What this lens evaluates
Character arc mechanics per K.M. Weiland: structure is the externalized shape of the internal journey. Every arc rests on one question — what does this character falsely believe about themselves or the world? If the Lie is unclear, every downstream beat feels disconnected.

## Criteria
- **The five-component chain, each causal to the next:** **Ghost** (the backstory wound that created the belief), **Lie** (the false belief — the belief, not the flaw; the flaw is behavior the Lie produces), **Want** (the external goal that serves the Lie and drives visible plot), **Need** (the truth that would free them, usually the Lie's opposite, invisible in Act 1), **Theme** (the Need stated as an arguable universal claim, not a sentiment). Want and Need must genuinely conflict: if getting the Want wouldn't threaten the Need, there is no tension.
- **Arc type identified with evidence:** Positive Change (Lie → Need accepted); Negative Change in three variants — the Fall (truth corrupted into a Lie), the Corruption (Need offered and rejected), the Disillusionment (idealistic Lie replaced by darker truth); Flat (hero holds a truth, the world embodies the Lie, secondary characters carry transformation).
- **Beat map against structural percentages, each beat doing its arc job:** Hook 1–5% (Lie visible in behavior, Ghost's shadow present); Inciting Event 10–12% (world disrupts the Lie-driven status quo); First Plot Point 20–25% (no return — hero enters the Lie-vs-Need arena); First Pinch Point 37–38% (antagonistic force shows the Lie's cost); Midpoint 50% (reactive → proactive, first genuine but inadequate move toward the Need); Second Pinch Point 62–63% (partial truth challenged, Lie fights back); Third Act Turning Point 75% (Lie appears to win completely, Ghost resurfaces); Climax 88–98% (hero actively CHOOSES the Need or rejects it); Resolution 98–100% (new normal under the arc's truth). Mark each Working / Weak / Missing.
- **Plot–arc fusion.** External events force internal reckoning with the Lie; the antagonist embodies or weaponizes the Lie.
- **Secondary arcs (up to four).** Each has its own Lie/Need in one line and a defined relation to the protagonist's arc — mirror, contrast, complication, cautionary, or catalyst — and reinforces the theme (same question, different answer).
- **Cascade logic.** A weak climax usually traces to a weak midpoint, which traces to a vague Lie, which traces to an undramatized Ghost. Diagnose upstream, not at the symptom.

## What to cite
The early scene where the Lie is visible in behavior; the Ghost's on-page evidence (dramatized or referenced); the First Plot Point door closing; the midpoint decision proving the reactive-to-proactive shift; the 75% beat where the Ghost resurfaces; the exact climactic choice; any beat landing far off its percentage with the structural consequence.

## Red flags
- No Lie — a fine person solving an external problem: no arc, no theme.
- Lie without Ghost — a false belief with no origin, reading as authorial convenience.
- Want and Need that never conflict.
- Midpoint where only the plot changes; the character stays reactive.
- 75% low point that isn't dark enough — visible paths forward remain, so the Lie was never fully tested.
- Climax resolved by what happens TO the hero rather than what the hero chooses.
- Plot and arc running as two parallel stories; antagonist unrelated to the Lie.
- Theme as unarguable sentiment ("love matters").

## Grading
- **Strong:** Clear Ghost→Lie→Want→Need→Theme chain, every landmark beat doing its arc job near its percentage, climax turning on an active choice about the Need, secondary arcs refracting the theme.
- **Solid:** Lie and Need present and the climax chooses, but the midpoint shift or pinch points are soft, or secondary arcs are disconnected.
- **Weak:** No discernible Lie, beats that advance plot without internal movement, passive resolution, theme stated rather than argued.
