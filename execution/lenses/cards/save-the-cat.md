# LENS: Save the Cat (BS2)
id: save-the-cat
source: skills/save-the-cat
applies_to: feature
role: structure
genres: all

## What this lens evaluates
Blake Snyder's Beat Sheet: whether the 15 beats land, on time and doing their jobs. Page targets assume a 110-page script — convert to percentages for other lengths (Catalyst ~10%, Break into Two ~23%, Midpoint 50%, All Is Lost ~68%, Break into Three ~77%). A beat that is late, missing, or doing two jobs at once is a locatable structural fault.

## Criteria
- **Opening Image (p.1) vs Final Image (p.110):** must be OPPOSITES proving change. If they don't mirror and invert, Act Two isn't adding up.
- **Theme Stated (~p.5):** the movie's argument voiced, usually by someone other than the hero.
- **Set-Up (pp.1–10):** every A-story character introduced or hinted; the Six Things That Need Fixing shown, not told; Stasis = Death; a **Save the Cat moment** that makes us like the hero ("cool" is not enough).
- **Catalyst by p.12 (~10%).** A definite external event, not a feeling. Later = Laying Pipe.
- **Debate (12–25):** posed as a question — should I go, can I do this?
- **Break into Two (p.25, ~23%):** the hero CHOOSES to enter the antithesis world. Not drifted, not tricked.
- **B Story (~p.30):** carries the theme. Always.
- **Fun and Games (30–55):** the **promise of the premise** — the trailer, why we came. If the middle abandons what the premise promised, the script breaks its contract.
- **Midpoint (p.55) / All Is Lost (p.75) axis:** false victory and false defeat must be INVERSES; stakes rise permanently at the midpoint; A and B stories cross there. All Is Lost must carry the **whiff of death**, literal or symbolic.
- **Dark Night of the Soul (75–85):** the hero beaten and knowing it, before the theme-driven **Break into Three (~p.85)** where A and B stories fuse.
- **Five-Point Finale:** Gathering the Team, Executing the Plan, the High Tower Surprise, **Dig Deep Down** (internal change drives external victory — no outside help, no deus ex machina), Executing the New Plan. Bad guys dispatched ascending; the Six Things paid off.
- **STC genre and its rules:** Monster in the House (monster + confined house + sin); Golden Fleece (the milestones must change the hero); Out of the Bottle (hero must eventually choose to give up the magic); Dude with a Problem (ordinary hero, primal problem, wins by individuality); Rites of Passage (unnamed life-monster; victory = acceptance); Buddy Love (incomplete halves; All Is Lost = separation); Whydunit (the WHY reveals human nature; audience changes, not hero); Fool Triumphant (fool + establishment + insider); Institutionalized (group vs. self; a breakout character and a newcomer); Superhero (special being, ordinary world — stress the pain of being special). Story genre, not setting genre: Alien is MITH, not sci-fi.
- **Immutable Laws:** Double Mumbo Jumbo (one piece of magic per movie); Pope in the Pool (exposition hidden under something watchable); Watch Out for That Glacier (threat must be immediate and escalating); Black Vet (edgy specificity that serves nothing); **Covenant of the Arc** (every character changes); Turn Turn Turn (every scene shifts + to − or − to +); the hero leads, never dragged.

## What to cite
Page (and %) for each of the 15 beats; the Save the Cat moment; the catalyst event; the choice at Break into Two; the whiff of death; the midpoint stake-raise; where the Five-Point Finale steps fall; each law violation with the offending page.

## Red flags
Catalyst past p.12 (Laying Pipe); hero waking up in Act Two without choosing it; Fun and Games that ignores the premise; midpoint and All Is Lost with the same polarity; All Is Lost with no death whiff; a finale won by outside help; B story that never touches theme; matching Opening/Final Images (no change); two pieces of magic; scenes that don't turn; a passive hero.

## Grading
- **Strong:** all 15 beats present within ~5% of target, opening/final images invert, midpoint/All Is Lost are true inverses, the finale is a five-point synthesis the hero earns, and no Immutable Law is broken.
- **Solid:** beats present but some drift or double duty; the axis works; one or two repairable law violations; promise of the premise mostly kept.
- **Weak:** hinge beats (Catalyst, Break into Two, Midpoint, All Is Lost, Break into Three) missing or inert, the hero is passive at the breaks, or the finale resolves by deus ex machina.
