# LENS: Grisanti Pilot Analysis
id: grisanti-pilot

applies_to: tv_pilot
role: core

## What this lens evaluates
The default core lens for every TV pilot. Jen Grisanti's goal/dilemma-centric diagnostic: does the pilot run the chain TRIGGER INCIDENT → DILEMMA → GOAL → escalating OBSTACLES → ALL IS LOST → the protagonist ACTIVELY resolving the goal? If something isn't working, the fault is usually upstream — an unclear trigger, a weak dilemma, or a muddy goal.

## Criteria
- **The core question**: can you state, from the pages alone, what the central character wants? If not, the pilot cannot work.
- **Logline test** (WHO + DILEMMA + ACTION + GOAL-with-irony): reconstruct the Series Logline, Pilot Logline, and Internal Logline from the script. A logline you cannot assemble marks a broken story.
- **Series Trigger vs Pilot Trigger**: identify both. Does the series-level event explain why we enter NOW, and does the pilot trigger flow from it?
- **Dilemma with two horns**: both options must be bad and both must be on the page (Breaking Bad: cook meth and risk jail vs die leaving nothing). One clear side = a problem, not a dilemma.
- **Goal clarity by end of Act 1**: external goal (what) and internal goal (why it matters emotionally) both established. The goal must stem from the dilemma.
- **Active protagonist**: they initiate, choose, and actively solve the goal in Act 4. Discoveries-only, deus ex machina, or things merely happening TO them = reactive.
- **Triangle of the Wound**: Wound (past trauma) → negative narrative (the lie they believe) → Flaw (visible self-sabotage) → Reaction. Does the trigger split the wound open? Does the flaw obstruct the goal?
- **Structure (Teaser + 4 acts)**: teaser hooks and shows the flaw; Act 2 escalates obstacles; Act 3 lands the All Is Lost (furthest from goal); Act 4 resolves and sets series stakes. Every act out must reflect back to the goal and end on the A story.
- **20-Minute Test**: is "why do I care" answered within ~20 pages, with stakes escalating act by act?
- **A/B/C stories**: each has its own trigger/dilemma/goal; the B story thematically elevates the A story; one theme ties them.
- **Series engine**: franchise, character, relationship, or mystery engine — can you see Season 4 and describe episode 2? If the series only starts in the last act, the pilot is backstory ("Move Act IV to Act I").
- **Voice**: where is the writer's truth fictionalized — especially at the All Is Lost moment in Acts 3–4?

## What to cite
The page where the pilot trigger lands; the lines establishing both horns of the dilemma; the moment the goal becomes explicit (with page number relative to the Act 1 out); each act-out beat and what it does to the goal; the visible flaw in behavior; the All Is Lost beat; the action by which the protagonist actively resolves; the scene/line that most reveals voice.

## Red flags
- Goal not clear by end of Act 1, or scenes that don't connect to it.
- Only one side of the dilemma dramatized.
- Act outs disconnected from the goal, or obstacles that don't escalate.
- Backstory dominating Acts 1–3; the series starting in the final act.
- Reactive hero; resolution arriving from outside.
- Thin character: no identifiable wound, no visible flaw, no negative narrative.
- Generic, "TV-like" pages with no moment of the writer's own truth.

## Grading
- **Strong**: dilemma with two real horns, goal crystal-clear by Act 1 out, every act out reflects the goal, active resolution, a visible engine that survives the Season 4 test, and real voice at the low point.
- **Solid**: chain intact but with slack — a soft horn of the dilemma, one or two act outs adrift, engine present but narrow, voice intermittent.
- **Weak**: the goal cannot be stated from the pages, the hero is reactive, obstacles don't escalate, or the pilot is setup/backstory with no visible series.
