# LENS: Grisanti Series Development
id: grisanti-series
source: skills/grisanti-series-development
applies_to: tv_pilot
role: structure
genres: all

## What this lens evaluates
Series-level architecture as evidenced by the pilot: whether this is a sustainable SERIES, not just a good episode. Grisanti's development framework read backwards — franchise/world, series trigger and dilemma, the engine, goal architecture across levels, and season-arc trajectory.

## Criteria
- **The World / Franchise**: is the world specific enough to generate endless story? Is there a hook that makes it unlike anything produced ("I've never seen THAT before")? Is there a central meeting place, and do place and time fuel the concept?
- **Format & Formula**: can you tell from the pilot what the show IS — procedural, serialized, or hybrid; case-of-the-week vs personal-journey A story; the professional/personal balance of a typical episode?
- **Series Trigger & Dilemma**: identify the macro inciting incident that sets the WHOLE series in motion and the impossible choice it forces. Both horns must be articulable in one sentence each — one clear side is a problem, not a dilemma.
- **The Engine** — name which engine(s) are on the page: **franchise** (the world generates stories), **character** (the wound/flaw creates recurring conflict), **relationship** (central relationships generate endless tension), **mystery** (an overarching question sustains). Strong shows run more than one.
- **Sustainability tests**: Can you see Season 4? What does episode #100 look like? Where do new stories come from each week? What keeps the central conflict from resolving too quickly or too slowly?
- **Goal architecture across levels**: the pilot goal should be one step toward a season-arc goal; external and internal goals exist at both series and pilot level; the strongest series goals carry irony (the character ends opposite where they started).
- **Season 1 arc**: does the pilot's ending set long-term stakes and a question/cliffhanger that brings the audience back? Is growth visible between where the character starts and ends?
- **Episodic viability (Episodes 2+)**: can self-contained episodes emerge that reference, but do not repeat, the pilot's backstory? Do the character dynamics introduced fuel NEW conflicts and let themes recur in fresh ways?
- **Character as renewable resource**: does the Triangle of the Wound (Wound → Flaw → Reaction) generate conflict for seasons, or is it resolvable in one episode?

## What to cite
The scene establishing the series trigger; the lines carrying both horns of the series dilemma; the beats that demonstrate the engine actually producing story (not just promising it); the pilot's closing beats that set season stakes; concrete world details that could each seed an episode; the moment the show's format/formula becomes legible.

## Red flags
- The series concept isn't clear by the end of the pilot; you cannot describe episode 2.
- Engine too narrow — the premise burns out once the pilot's question is answered.
- World is generic backdrop; the hook is a situation, not a franchise.
- Series dilemma has one horn, or the trigger explains nothing about why NOW.
- All backstory, series starting in the last act ("Move Act IV to Act I").
- No question at the pilot's end pulling the audience to episode 2.
- Central conflict either resolves in the pilot or visibly can never move.

## Grading
- **Strong**: a named, working engine (ideally two) visible in the pilot's own beats; both horns of the series dilemma dramatized; a clear formula for typical episodes; Season 4 is easy to imagine.
- **Solid**: engine present but under-fed — the world or relationships could generate story, but the pilot demonstrates little of it; season arc implied rather than staked.
- **Weak**: a movie premise in pilot clothing — no visible engine, no episodic formula, series dilemma absent or one-sided, no reason to return for episode 2.
