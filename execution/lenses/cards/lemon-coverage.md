# LENS: Lemon Coverage
id: lemon-coverage
source: skills/lemon-coverage
applies_to: both
role: core
genres: all

## What this lens evaluates
The house method. One question: does this script deserve to be made? PASS means "do not make this even if it costs two pesos." RECOMMEND means "I do not care what it costs. Make this script." Budget, market, castability are out of scope — if the script is failing, the reason is on the page. Judge the writing against the best film in its genre, never against the pile.

## Criteria
- **Evidence rule.** Every claim, positive or negative, carries a page number. No cite, no claim.
- **Comparison rule.** Judge against the named comparable that did this thing right — the unflattering comp, the film this script must beat — one line each on stronger and weaker.
- **Narrowness rule.** Do not penalize a script for being narrow and sharp; check whether the specificity IS the asset before judging it inaccessible.
- **Label claims.** Separate fact-on-the-page (with cite), inference, and judgment. Never blend them.
- **The Read.** Where attention pulled forward, drifted, predicted the next beat, and where you would have stopped watching at home — with pages. Name the movie it is trying to be; does it become that movie?
- **Genre contract.** Score each convention and obligatory scene DELIVERED (cite + comparison), GESTURED AT (present, not doing its job), or ABSENT. No page number = ABSENT. The Core Event (the scene the audience came for) must be fully played on the page.
- **Character.** Moral blind spot in one sentence; **immoral effect** — the flaw must hurt someone else on the page, with cite, or it is a quirk; want vs. need (if identical, no arc); **agency test** — three cited scenes where the protagonist drives the plot; **earned vs. asserted arc** — cite the closing action they could not have done at the start. Opponent must attack the protagonist's greatest weakness and want the same thing.
- **Audience gauntlet.** The promise in audience language (laugh with my family, scream with friends, cry and feel seen — not genre labels); originality is HOW the promise is delivered, never a substitute for one. The one sentence that makes a stranger lean in (two sentences = two premises fighting). The **unforgettable image**, with page — none means no center of gravity, a writing failure. The urgency test: why would someone feel stupid for missing this? Emotional experience act by act. How many roles would a great actor fight for? Does the title promise the film the script delivers?
- **The Kill Case.** The strongest good-faith argument for passing, even on a script being recommended. If it beats the verdict, the verdict is wrong.
- **Primary risk type.** Name one: Concept (two premises fighting / too familiar), Execution (only works if made perfectly — the most under-diagnosed), Tone (promises one feeling, delivers another), Second act (forty pages stretched to a hundred), Ending (climax cannot satisfy the premise's promise).
- **Falsifiability.** Every verdict states what on the page would move it up a grade.

## What to cite
Pages for every observation; the drift/stop points from the straight read; each obligatory scene's page or absence; the three agency scenes; the immoral-effect moment; the arc's start and landing actions; the unforgettable image's page; a named comparable per judgment.

## Red flags
Opening with strengths (concerns lead, always); softeners ("could benefit from," "a lot to like"); praising intent ("ambitious") or a scene for merely existing; a strong first act buying goodwill for a collapsed third; explaining a craft failure via cost or market; defending a script on craft alone — **if the only case for a screenplay is the writing, it has no reason to exist**; "has potential" as a verdict; a CONSIDER without a scoped rewrite (that is a PASS); an unfalsifiable verdict.

## Grading
- **Strong (RECOMMEND):** Core Event delivered, ~80%+ obligatory scenes delivered, agency passed with an earned arc, protagonist-driven climax, a citable unforgettable image. Rare — more than one in five means the bar drifted.
- **Solid (CONSIDER):** The material is genuinely there; requires a specific rewrite nameable in one sentence.
- **Weak (PASS):** A kill fires — can't name the movie it wants to be, Core Event absent, agency failed with an asserted arc, externally resolved climax — or the repair exceeds commissioning a new script. Say so plainly.
