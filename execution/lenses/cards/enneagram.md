# LENS: Enneagram Character Analysis
id: enneagram
source: skills/enneagram-analyst
applies_to: both
role: character
genres: all

## What this lens evaluates
Whether each major character behaves like a coherent psychology — a specific Enneagram type at a specific Level of Development — and whether the arc, opponent, allies, and relationships are built on that psychology or merely asserted. Method: Jeff Lyons' Rapid Story Development plus the Enneagram Institute's nine Levels.

## Criteria
- **Type is inferable from behavior.** Locate the Center first — Head (5,6,7: anxiety, "what if"), Heart (2,3,4: shame, image), Body (8,9,1: anger, control) — then the type via Core Fear / Core Desire / Survival Strategy / Blind Spot. Types: 1 Reformer (fear of being corrupt), 2 Helper (unloved), 3 Achiever (worthless), 4 Individualist (no identity), 5 Investigator (helpless), 6 Loyalist (without support), 7 Enthusiast (trapped/in pain), 8 Challenger (controlled/harmed), 9 Peacemaker (loss/separation). Decisions under pressure, what is avoided or pursued obsessively, and speech patterns must all point to the same type.
- **Wing consistency.** Does one adjacent type consistently flavor the behavior (e.g., 8w7 Maverick vs 8w9 Bear), or does the character drift arbitrarily?
- **Level placement.** Where does the character operate — healthy 1–3 (self-aware, flexible), average 4–6 (defensive, survival strategy running), unhealthy 7–9 (blind spot in full control)? Fluctuation of 1–2 levels is human; larger unmotivated jumps are inconsistency.
- **Stress/growth directions honored.** Under pressure the character should move toward the type's disintegration point (1→4, 2→8, 3→9, 4→2, 5→7, 6→3, 7→1, 8→5, 9→6); growth toward integration (1→7, 2→4, 3→6, 4→1, 5→8, 6→9, 7→5, 8→2, 9→3).
- **Arc trajectory readable in levels.** Positive = up-levels via a crisis that confronts the blind spot; negative = down-levels via rationalized compromises until the character becomes what they feared; flat = level held while catalyzing others; tragic = clarity arriving after irreversible consequences. The doom moment must hit the type-specific low, and the climax must contain a moment of truth about the blind spot; the resolution must show changed behavior, not stated change.
- **Opponent design.** Does the antagonist target the protagonist's blind spots, distortion filters, and pinches, embody the de-evolution point, and block the evolution path?
- **Ally/mentor design.** Does someone model the evolution point or supply wing resources? Is the mentor a healthy version of the type or its growth point? Are false allies enabling the blind spot?
- **Ensemble variety.** Mixed Harmonic Triads — Positive Outlook (2,7,9), Reactive (4,6,8), Competency (1,3,5) — give varied responses to the same crisis; type clustering breeds monotony.

## What to cite
Scenes and pages showing: the decision that reveals core fear/desire; the survival strategy in action; the pressure scene demonstrating stress-direction behavior; the doom moment; the climactic confrontation with the blind spot; dialogue exhibiting the type's speech markers ("should/ought" for 1s, worst-case questioning for 6s, qualifiers for 9s); the opponent exploiting a named pinch.

## Red flags
- Transformation without ever confronting the blind spot — change is declared, not earned.
- Behavior that jumps types scene to scene to serve plot convenience.
- Doom moment generic to any character rather than this type's specific low point.
- Decline behavior that ignores the disintegration direction.
- Opponent who never touches the protagonist's psychology — external obstacle only.
- No character modeling the evolution point anywhere in the cast.
- Resolution restating the theme in dialogue instead of demonstrating a level shift in action.

## Grading
- **Strong:** Every major character types cleanly from on-page behavior; the arc moves levels through a blind-spot crisis; opponent and allies are built from the protagonist's de-evolution and evolution points.
- **Solid:** Protagonist types cleanly and the arc tracks, but wings/levels blur, or supporting cast is psychologically generic and under-leveraged.
- **Weak:** Characters are untypeable bundles of plot-required behavior; arcs happen to them without psychological cause; opponent is unrelated to the protagonist's inner pattern.
