# LENS: Story Grid

source: skills/story-grid-expert

## What this lens evaluates
Shawn Coyne's editorial diagnostic: genre as a promise to the audience, and whether the script keeps it. Identify the External and Internal Content Genres, verify conventions and obligatory moments are ON THE PAGE, and track value change from scene to global story.

## Criteria
- **Name the genres.** External Content Genre (conscious object of desire) in one word; Internal (subconscious need) likewise. A shifting object of desire means an unfocused genre — that is the diagnosis.
- **Four Core Framework:** Core Need, Core Value, Core Emotion, and the **Core Event** — the must-have climactic scene (Horror: victim at mercy of monster; Thriller/Action: hero at mercy of villain; Love: proof of love; Crime: exposure of the criminal; Morality/Status: the big choice; Performance: the big performance). It must be dramatized on stage, never reported.
- **Conventions vs. obligatory moments.** Conventions set up expectations (setting constraints, avatars, catalysts: clock, MacGuffin, point of no return); obligatory moments pay them off. Every one must be on the page — not implied.
- **Five Commandments, at global level and in every unit:** (1) Inciting Incident (causal or coincidental; globally it must upset the balance, contain something not understood, and point at the ending); (2) Progressive Complications delivered through Turning Points, Active or Revelatory — each crisis harder to reverse than the last, toward the limits of human experience; (3) Crisis as a genuine dilemma — **Best Bad Choice or Irreconcilable Goods**; an easy or obvious option is a broken crisis; (4) Climax — the protagonist ACTS on the choice, on stage; (5) Resolution — a genuinely new equilibrium, not a summary.
- **Beginning Hook / Middle Build / Ending Payoff at ~25/50/25**, each with its own Five Commandments — the **15 skeletal scenes**. The Middle Build must break the hero's initial strategy.
- **Value progression to the end of the line:** positive → contrary → negative → **negation of the negation** (Crime: justice → unfairness → injustice → tyranny; Love: love → desire → hate → self-hatred masquerading as love; Status: success → compromise → failure → selling out). BH moves the global value one level, MB further, EP to the end of the line. Act climaxes must turn on the GLOBAL value, not a secondary one.
- **Every scene turns.** A definable value shift from opening to close, with an identifiable turning point. If no one-sentence event synthesis can be written ("[ending value] when [protagonist acts] despite [tradeoff]"), the scene has no turn — it is exposition, however well written.
- **A story vs. B story:** external plot vs. internal theme; the deepest scripts win one and lose the other (irony). Controlling Idea statable as `[VALUE] prevails/fails when [ACTION]`, and every scene serves it.
- **Innovation test:** genre dictates WHAT must happen; originality lives in HOW. An obligatory moment executed as the first thing anyone would think of is a cliché even when present.

## What to cite
Pages of the global Inciting Incident, each obligatory moment, the Core Event, and the 15 skeletal scenes; per sampled scene, opening and closing value and the turning point; where the value hits negation of the negation; the on-stage climax choice.

## Red flags
A genre you cannot name in one word; Core Event missing, offstage, or cut away from; crises with an easy out; complications repeating at one level instead of escalating; flat scenes with no value shift; a Middle Build that only fills time; an EP that never reaches the end of the line; act climaxes turning on secondary values; subplots competing with the Controlling Idea; a protagonist unchanged at the end.

## Grading
- **Strong:** genres named cleanly; all conventions and obligatory moments on the page with fresh execution; 15 skeletal scenes present; global value reaches negation of the negation; every sampled scene turns; A/B stories create ironic meaning.
- **Solid:** genre clear and Core Event delivered, but some obligatory moments gestured at, escalation uneven, or a stretch of flat scenes; repairable within this structure.
- **Weak:** genre unfocused, Core Event absent or offstage, crises broken, or the global value never progresses — the promise to the audience goes unpaid.
