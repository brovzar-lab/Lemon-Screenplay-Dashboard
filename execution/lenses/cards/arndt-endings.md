# LENS: Arndt Endings
id: arndt-endings
source: skills/arndt-endings
applies_to: both
role: structure
genres: all

## What this lens evaluates
Whether the ending is engineered to land, per Michael Arndt's "Insanely Great Endings" model: three sets of stakes converging in a roughly two-minute climax where underdog values overturn dominant values. Rate the ending on the quality ladder — BAD (positive + predictable), GOOD (positive + surprising), INSANELY GREAT (positive + surprising + meaningful). "Positive" is a framing, not a requirement: the same machine produces devastating endings (8 1/2). Apply as one precise model, not a law all endings must obey.

## Criteria
- **Three sets of stakes, each with its own complete arc** (setup in Act 1, deepened in Act 2, paid off at the climax): **External** (the visible plot question — survival, the contest, the deadline), **Internal/emotional** (love, friendship, self-respect), **Philosophical** (a contest between two value systems — the most neglected set, and where meaning lives). Mark each Working, Thin, or Missing; check the philosophical set hardest.
- **Dominant vs underdog values nameable in plain language.** Dominant = the governing ethos of the story's world, efficient and cynical; underdog = the protagonist's more human, dismissible alternative (Star Wars: coercion vs the Force; Little Miss Sunshine: winners-and-losers vs being yourself). If both cannot be named from the page, the ending will resolve plot without producing meaning. Name them in the story's own cultural register, not the abstract.
- **The cast of the contest.** An antagonist who is the dominant values' spokesperson and gets an **Antagonist's Aria** — a scene of being persuasively, temptingly right (Tarkin, Mr. Potter); a mentor whose **Mentor's Aria** plants the seed the hero acts on at the climax; a closest ally with a **Judas moment** (choosing dominant values, deepening despair) and a return folded into the climax (Han Solo).
- **The Death Star location.** The climax is staged in the physical apotheosis of the dominant values — the church wedding, the pageant stage — not somewhere neutral or convenient.
- **Moment of Despair.** Just before the turn, all three stakes appear irretrievably lost. Mandatory contrast; without it the reversal has no dark background.
- **Decisive Act.** One single, active choice BY the protagonist, embracing the underdog values against self-interest, at a cost (targeting computer off; pounding the glass). Not a rescue by circumstance.
- **Convergence.** External, internal, and philosophical resolutions collapse into the same ~two minutes, the same space, causally triggered by the Decisive Act — not scattered across three separate scenes. This is the line between good and insanely great.
- **The Emotional Promise.** The opening writes a specific emotional check; the ending must cash that exact check, not a different one.

## What to cite
The scene/pages of the intended climax; where each of the three stakes is set up and paid off; the aria scenes (quote the antagonist's best argument); the Judas beat and return; the despair beat; the exact action constituting the Decisive Act; the page spread across which the three resolutions land (proximity is the evidence for convergence).

## Red flags
- The "so what?" ending: external and internal resolved, no philosophical stake.
- Toothless antagonist — evil but never persuasive; beating them settles no argument.
- Passive climax: the ending happens TO the hero.
- Scattered climax: three resolutions in three scenes, energy leaking between them.
- Wrong location: a neutral venue instead of the Death Star.
- No despair — the win comes easy.
- Theme delivered as a speech instead of a value contest with a won-or-lost outcome.
- Ending that answers a different emotional question than the opening posed.

## Grading
- **Strong:** All three stakes arc and converge on one Decisive Act in the Death Star, after a true Moment of Despair, with a persuasive aria on each side — the win inverts the story world's moral order.
- **Solid:** External and internal converge and surprise, but the philosophical stake is thin or implied — satisfying, forgettable.
- **Weak:** Predictable or passive resolution, no nameable value contest, stakes resolved in scattered beats.
