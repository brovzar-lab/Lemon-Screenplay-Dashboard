# LENS: Truby Story Anatomy
id: truby
source: skills/truby-expert
applies_to: both
role: structure
genres: all

## What this lens evaluates
John Truby's organic structure: story as character change made visible through action. Structure grows from weakness-need. Test whether the chain weakness → desire → opponent → plan → battle → self-revelation is causal and intact, whether the opposition puts maximum pressure on the hero's flaw, and whether theme is argued through the characters rather than tacked on.

## Criteria
- **Weakness-Need (the foundation).** A **psychological weakness** (hurting self) and a **moral weakness** (hurting others, on the page). The audience sees it; the hero does not. If weakness-need is wrong, everything built on it collapses. The hero must be FASCINATING (weakness + desire), not likeable.
- **Ghost and Story World.** The past event that installed the weakness, and a world that expresses and pressures the hero's condition (in horror the ghost is the most important beat — the haunted house is the flawed mind made physical; comedy's engine is the **comic gap**, the pretense-high the character keeps being dropped from).
- **Desire.** One specific goal with a clear endpoint that forces direct conflict. In comedy, a **dangerous desire** whose trouble is all the hero's fault.
- **Opponent.** The best opponent is the character most like the hero, **competes for the same goal**, and **attacks the hero's greatest weakness**. Check both. Also note any **fake-ally opponent** (betrayal that tests trust).
- **Plan and counterattack.** The hero has a strategy; the opponent's plan is usually more ruthless. The **drive** is both adjusting to each other — not the hero merely enduring events.
- **Attack by Ally.** Someone who loves the hero names the moral flaw — a key hinge of moral growth; its absence usually means the moral line was never dramatized.
- **Apparent Defeat, then obsessive drive.** The hero hits bottom, then pushes desperately on new information (revelation/decision beats). Look for the **Gate/Gauntlet/Visit to Death** before the battle.
- **Battle.** The final conflict settles the goal; the hero must use everything learned.
- **Self-Revelation, doubled.** Psychological (who am I really?) AND moral (how have I treated others?). Then the **Moral Decision** — the action proving the change. A revelation stated in dialogue but never proven by a costly choice is not a revelation.
- **New Equilibrium.** A genuinely new level, higher or lower. Name the arc type: child→adult, adult→leader, cynic→participant, leader→tyrant, leader→visionary.
- **Four-Corner Opposition.** Hero, main opponent, and two secondary opponents compete for the same goal, each attacking the weakness differently, each arguing a different answer to the moral question. Two characters holding the same position is a wasted corner.
- **Moral argument, not message.** Theme is argued through the drive in action and dialogue; the self-revelation concludes it. **Organic unity:** every beat serves character change, theme, AND plot at once; a beat serving only plot is mechanical.
- **Premise integrity.** One line containing change, desire, opponent, and battle; a **designing principle** — HOW this story uniquely unfolds, not just what happens.

## What to cite
The scenes establishing psychological and moral weakness (the moral one must cost someone); the ghost; the desire's declaration; evidence the opponent wants the same thing and targets the flaw; the ally's attack; the apparent defeat; the battle; the self-revelation and the moral decision proving it — all by page.

## Red flags
A wound with no moral weakness (nobody gets hurt); an opponent merely evil, unlike the hero, or after a different goal; a drive of episodic events with no mutual adjustment; a self-revelation as speech with no proving action; theme stated by a mouthpiece; opposition corners missing or duplicated; a world that is backdrop, not pressure.

## Grading
- **Strong:** the seven-step chain is causal and complete, the opponent is a same-goal dark mirror, four corners argue distinct moral positions, and the moral decision proves the self-revelation on the page.
- **Solid:** chain intact but a link is thin — soft moral weakness, missing ally attack, a battle under-testing the change.
- **Weak:** weakness-need absent or purely psychological, opponent generic or goal-disjoint, self-revelation asserted — mechanical structure.
