# LENS: Women in Story Audit
id: women-in-story
source: skills/women-in-story-audit
applies_to: both
role: audit
genres: all

## What this lens evaluates
How women exist, act, relate, speak, and shape the audience's experience of the story — named representation tests as diagnostic lenses, then the craft beneath them. Evidence-based only: report what the page establishes and what it cannot. Never infer gender, race, sexuality, disability, or other identity from names, appearance, accent, or stereotype.

## Criteria
- **Character map.** Every woman with dialogue, recurring presence, or a material story function: role, goal, relationships, POV access, major decisions.
- **Four core tests, each reported Pass / Fail / Indeterminate / Not assessable — never an aggregate score:**
  - **Bechdel-Wallace:** at least two women talk to each other about something other than a man (say whether the original or later named-character convention is used). Measures minimal presence only.
  - **Mako Mori:** at least one female character has her own narrative arc not about supporting a man's story; "own arc" means change driven by her problem, desire, decisions, and consequences — competence or plot participation alone is not an arc.
  - **Ko:** a non-white, female-identifying person speaks in five or more scenes (its English criterion targets a U.S. media pattern; flag its limits for non-English work).
  - **Villarreal:** initial fail if the lead woman is introduced as sexualized, hardened/soulless, or a tired/overworked matriarch; redeemed if she later shows at least three of: career authority, motherhood, recklessness/bad decisions, owned sexuality. Report the formal result, then judge the craft.
  - Optional when relevant: **Peirce** (her own story, authentic desires, understandable action), **Sexy Lamp** (replaceable by an attractive object?), **Sphinx**, **Vito Russo**, **Villalobos**.
- **Craft beneath the tests:** **Agency** (what each major woman wants, chooses, initiates, risks, refuses, causes); **Interiority** (private desire, contradiction, why she acts); **Consequence** (do her choices materially alter plot, relationships, meaning); **Relationships** (distinct dynamics with other women, not merely parallel relationships to men); **Voice** (speaking scenes, partners, subjects, who carries key information); **Perspective** (whose experience organizes scenes — who perceives, interprets, remembers, receives aftermath); **Portrayal** (stereotypes, sexualization, competence without humanity, suffering used only to motivate others); **Intersectionality** only where the text supplies evidence.
- **Interpretive discipline:** a pass proves neither quality nor feminism; a fail proves neither badness nor sexism. Agency is not combat skill, likability, or winning. Romance, motherhood, vulnerability, and culturally specific roles are not automatic failures. Distinguish constrained choices from no choices, dialogue quantity from importance, plot indispensability from interiority. Respect premise, tone, and genre while naming what is on the page.

## What to cite
Scene/page for every test result; each major woman's introduction description (quote how the page describes her vs comparable men); decision scenes proving or disproving agency and consequence; woman-to-woman conversations and their subjects; scenes organized around her perception or aftermath. Use Not assessable when the format lacks the evidence (e.g., dialogue tests on an outline).

## Red flags
- Sexy Lamp failure: a woman replaceable by an object, reward, or clue.
- A woman's suffering or death functioning only as a man's motivation.
- Women who exist only in parallel relationships to men, never to each other.
- Introductions foregrounding attractiveness or age where men get profession or intent.
- Competence without humanity — skilled, but no desire, error, contradiction.
- Test results without citable scene evidence; identity inferred from stereotype.

## Grading
- **Strong:** Core tests pass on clear evidence, and major women show agency, interiority, and consequence — their choices drive the plot and the story grants them perspective.
- **Solid:** Tests mostly pass but women are present more than causal — thin interiority or woman-to-woman relationships, perspective anchored elsewhere.
- **Weak:** Core tests fail or pass only technically while women function as objects, rewards, or motivation, with no scene organized around a woman's experience.
